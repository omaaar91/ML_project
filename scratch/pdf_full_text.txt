The story
You have just joined the data team at Nile Connect, a mobile and home-internet operator in Egypt. The CEO
has three worries and one question for you:
  Too many customers are leaving. About 1 in 4 customers in our sample has left. Who is next?
 Billing is a black box. Why does one customer pay 200 EGP and another 600 EGP?
 One offer for everyone does not work. We send the same SMS to all customers. Who are our
customers, really?
 Our bundles are random. Which services do people buy together?
Your job: use everything you learned in the Machine Learning module to answer these four questions with
data, and write a short, clear recommendation the CEO can act on.
Think like a data scientist, not like a student
A model with a high score is not the goal. A decision the business can trust is the goal. In every part, ask yourself:
"So what? What would we do differently on Monday morning?"
What you will practice
 Topic from the course
Where you use it
Linear Regression (one + multiple variables)
Part 2 - predict the monthly bill
R2, RMSE, MAE
Part 2 - judge the bill models
Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, SVM, KNN, Naive
Bayes
Part 3 - predict churn
Ensemble Learning: Random Forest, XGBoost
Part 3 + 4 - stronger churn models
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score

Cross Validation, GridSearchCV, RandomizedSearchCV

Clustering: KMeans, HAC, DBSCAN

Time: about 10-14 hours of work. Work alone or in pairs. Tools: Python, pandas, scikit-learn, xgboost, mlxtend, matplotlib /
seaborn.

PART 1 · 10 POINTS
Know your data and clean it
Files: customers.csv, usage_behavior.csv, service_transactions.csv
 1.1
Load the three files. For each one print shape, data types, missing values per column and basic statistics.
Write 3-5 lines on what you see.
 1.2
Data audit. Make a small table: problem found | which file / column | how many rows | how you fixed it. Look
for missing values, duplicate rows, impossible or extreme values, and text that is written in different ways.
Hint: Check value_counts() of every text column. Look at the max of every number column.
 1.3
Clean the data. Choose a different rule for each type of problem (drop, median, mode, cap, fix text) and
explain why in one sentence each.
 1.4
Explore. Draw at least 5 charts. Required: churn rate by contract_type, churn rate by tenure group, and a
correlation heatmap of the numeric columns. Write one business sentence under each chart.
 1.5
Look at the churn column. What percent of customers left? Why can this make accuracy a dangerous
metric? Answer in 3 lines.
Rule
Every cleaning choice must be written down. Data leakage rule: fit anything that learns from data (median, scaler,
encoder) on the training set only. For Part 1, simple cleaning before the split is fine; for Parts 3-4, use a Pipeline.

 2.1

PART 3 · 20 POINTS
Who will leave? (Classification)
Target: churn · File: customers.csv
 3.1
Prepare the data: drop customer_id and the target from X, turn churn into 0/1, encode categories, split 80/20
with stratify=y and random_state=42. Keep the test set untouched until Part 4 ends.
 3.2
Train these 7 models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, SVM, KNN, Naive Bayes, Random Forest,
XGBoost. Use default settings first.
Hint: SVM, KNN and Logistic Regression need scaled data. Trees and XGBoost do not. Use a Pipeline so
scaling is learned from training data only.
 3.3
For each model report Accuracy, Precision, Recall and F1 for the churn class. Show them in one table,
sorted by F1.
 3.4
Draw the confusion matrix of the best 2 models. Explain in your own words: what is a False Negative here,
and what is a False Positive? Which one costs the company more?
 3.5
Business cost. Assume: losing a customer costs 1,500 EGP; a retention offer to one customer costs 200
EGP. Using confusion matrices, calculate the money impact of the 3 best models. Is the best-F1 model also
the best-money model?
 3.6
Show feature importance from Random Forest and XGBoost. Do they agree? Write the top 5 reasons
customers leave, in simple language.
Lived-experience tip
A model that says "nobody will churn" gets about 74% accuracy on this data - and is completely useless. Always
compare against this dumb baseline before you celebrate any score.

PART 4 · 15 POINTS
Fair comparison and tuning
Cross Validation · GridSearchCV · RandomizedSearchCV
 4.1
Use 5-fold Stratified Cross Validation on the training set for all 7 models. Report mean and standard
deviation of F1. Which model is the most stable, not only the highest?
 4.2
GridSearchCV on one model of your choice (Decision Tree or SVM are good). Define a grid of at least 3
parameters, score with F1, cv=5. Show the best parameters and the best score.
 4.3
RandomizedSearchCV on Random Forest or XGBoost with at least 5 parameters and n_iter of 30 or more.
Measure the time it took.
 4.4
Compare Grid and Randomized search: time, score, number of combinations tried. When would you pick
each one in a real project?
 4.5
Choose one final model. Evaluate it on the untouched test set one time only. Report all four metrics and
compare with the cross-validation score. Are they close? If not, what could be wrong?
 4.6
Bonus idea (not required): try class_weight='balanced' or a different decision threshold (for example 0.35
instead of 0.5). Does recall improve? What do you lose?
Common mistake
Tuning on the test set. If you check the test score, change something, and check again, the test set is no longer a
test. It becomes part of training and your final number is too optimistic.

PART 5 · 20 POINTS
Find the hidden customer segments (Clustering +
PCA)
File: usage_behavior.csv · no labels
 5.1
Prepare: drop customer_id, handle missing values, and scale all features with StandardScaler. Write one
sentence: why must we scale before KMeans, HAC and DBSCAN?
 5.2
KMeans. Try k from 2 to 8. Plot the elbow curve and the silhouette score. Choose k and defend your choice.
Then profile each cluster (mean of each feature).
 5.3
HAC. Draw a dendrogram (use Ward linkage first). Cut it into the same number of clusters. Try at least one
other linkage (average or complete). Do the groups match KMeans?
 5.4
DBSCAN. Choose eps with a k-distance plot and justify min_samples. How many clusters and how many
noise points do you get? Look at the noise points: who are these customers?
Hint: If you get one giant cluster or 100% noise, your eps is wrong. Try again with the plot.
 5.5
PCA. Fit PCA on the scaled data. Plot explained variance (cumulative). How many components keep about
90% of the information? Draw a 2D scatter plot colored by your best clustering.
 5.6
Run KMeans again on the PCA output (the components you kept). Do you get the same segments? What
did you gain and what did you lose?
 5.7
Name your segments. Give each cluster a business name (for example "Night Gamers") and write 2 lines
on what offer fits each one.
